What Size Generator Do I Need to Run a Washing Machine?

Published October 2, 2026.

Use the higher start on the two charts, then see whether one inverter's maximum covers it.

Quick answer: Honda lists an automatic washer at 1,200 running watts and 1,200 starting watts, and Champion lists a washing machine at 500–1,000 running watts and 1,000–2,300 starting watts. Covering both takes a running rating that clears 1,200 W and a starting rating that clears 2,300 W. A Honda EU2200i maximum of 2,200 W is just under that 2,300 W start, while two EU2200i units in parallel reach up to 4,400 W and the Champion manual's 6,250 W starting rating also covers it.

How to size it

Add the running watts of every load that will be on together, then add the single highest starting surge (Generac; Honda). Generac notes that starting watts are often 2–3 times running watts, and that standby units should get a dealer survey.

The washer motor is the surge. An electric dryer on the same circuit plan is a different problem: Honda lists it at 5,400 W running. Honda notes that an inverter converts AC to DC and back to AC, which gives lower THD than a conventional generator that runs at full RPM, which matters if the washer's controls are electronic.

FAQ

Can a Honda EU2200i run a washing machine?

Honda's own washer row is 1,200 W running and starting, under the 1,800 W rated output. Champion's upper start is 2,300 W, which is over the 2,200 W maximum.

What running watts should I use?

1,200 W covers Honda and sits above Champion's 500–1,000 W running range.

Can the washer and an electric dryer run together?

The dryer alone is 5,400 W running and 6,750 W starting at 240 V. Add the washer only after the dryer figure is covered.

Does Champion list a start above the running watts?

Yes. Champion lists 1,000–2,300 starting watts against 500–1,000 running watts.
