Which generator is right for me?

Published October 3, 2026.

Built by Jeremy Panasuk.

The right class is the one that clears your list. The path below starts at 2,000 W and only leaves that class when a published row forces it.

Quick answer: Honda's Energy Star fridge at 192 W running / 1,200 W start, plus a 1/3 hp furnace fan at 700 W running / 1,400 W start, marked 240 V, sizes to 2,000 W. That is a portable recommendation. It is not a 20 kW standby. Add a 4-ton air conditioner at the top of Champion's band and the same formula leaves the 2,000 W class. Enter the rows on the calculator before you shop.

The list that is actually 2,000 W

  • Running total = 192 + 700 = 892 W.
  • Largest start is the furnace fan at 1,400 W, which is higher than the fridge's 1,200 W. Extra surge = 1,400 − 700 = 700 W.
  • Peak = 892 + 700 = 1,592 W.
  • 1,592 × 1.20 = 1,910 W, rounded up to the next 500 W = 2,000 W recommended.

Honda marks that furnace-fan row 240 V. A 120 V inverter can cover the fridge's 1,200 W start and still miss the fan, because the fan is not a 120 V load on Honda's chart. Branches that change the class

You are only keeping food cold and a few small 120 V loads. Stay with the fridge page and the camping list. A fan, a laptop, and a radio size to 1,000 W on best portable generator for camping. A battery with a published watt-hour rating, matched to the same 192 W fridge, is on best solar generator. That page is one sheet, not a test.

You want the furnace fan, and it is the 240 V row. The generator needs 240 V output. The watt recommendation is still 2,000 W until you add more rows. A transfer switch is required if those loads are on household circuits. Cord-versus-switch is on backup generator installation.

A common mistake

Buying a 20 kW standby because a neighbor did, while the list in front of you is a fridge and a furnace fan. Those two rows are a 2,000 W recommendation. The 20 kW sheet is the right comparison only after the load list, the 240 V loads, and the wish for automatic start are actually on the table. The other mistake is dropping the furnace fan's 240 V mark and shopping a 120 V inverter whose watt rating happens to say 2,200.

Limits, and when to call a pro

Nameplates beat chart rows. If the fan motor is not the 1/3 hp row, do not keep 700 and 1,400. Re-enter the plate on the calculator. Connecting any of these sets to a panel takes a licensed electrician and a permit where local code requires one.
