10 kW generator for a home

Published October 3, 2026.

Built by Jeremy Panasuk.

10 kW is the recommendation for one published pair. It is not a whole-house default.

Quick answer: Champion's 4-ton central air conditioner, taken at the top of the published band (5,000 W running, 8,000 W starting), plus Honda's Energy Star fridge at 192 W running and 1,200 W starting, sizes to 10,000 W with this site's 20% headroom. The peak the generator has to start is 8,192 W. Add Honda's 4,500 W hot-water heater and the recommendation becomes 15,500 W.

The list that lands on 10,000 W

The 4-ton page already records Champion's range: 4,000–5,000 running watts and 6,500–8,000 starting watts. Using the top of a published range is a choice, stated here, not a measurement of your condenser. Honda's fridge row is 132–192 W running and 1,200 W starting. This case uses 192 W. Both on together, headroom 20%:

  • Running total = 5,000 + 192 = 5,192 W.
  • Largest start is the air conditioner at 8,000 W. Extra surge = 8,000 − 5,000 = 3,000 W. The fridge's own gap, 1,200 − 192 = 1,008 W, is smaller, so the formula does not add it.
  • Peak = 5,192 + 3,000 = 8,192 W.
  • 8,192 × 1.20 = 9,830 W, rounded up to the next 500 W = 10,000 W recommended.

A generator whose running rating clears 10,000 W and whose starting rating clears 8,192 W matches this list. A unit that only advertises 10,000 starting watts, with a lower running rating, does not satisfy the buy number. The condenser is hardwired. Honda does not need to be consulted for that sentence; the 4-ton page already treats the condenser as a hardwired load, which means a transfer switch or interlock installed by an electrician.

Gasoline at 50% of the 10,000 W recommendation is 10,000 / 7,000 gallons per hour. A 4 gallon tank lasts 2.8 hours. That short time is the portable fuel rule applied to a large recommendation. It is a reason this pair is an awkward fit for a small gasoline tank, not a standby fuel test. Propane at 1.37 times that gasoline rate lasts 2.0 hours on the same 4 gallons. The factors are on how much propane a generator uses.

The row that leaves 10 kW

Honda lists a hot water heater at 4,500 running watts and 4,500 starting watts, marked 240 V. Add it to the same air conditioner and fridge:

  • Running total = 5,192 + 4,500 = 9,692 W.
  • Largest start is still the air conditioner at 8,000 W, because 8,000 is above the water heater's 4,500. Extra surge stays 3,000 W.
  • Peak = 9,692 + 3,000 = 12,692 W.
  • 12,692 × 1.20 = 15,230 W, rounded up to the next 500 W = 15,500 W recommended.

15,500 W is above a 10,000 W nameplate. The peak, 12,692 W, is also above 10,000 W, so this is not a case where headroom alone pushes you over and the peak still fits. The water heater is a resistive load: Honda prints the same number for start and run, and the formula adds no extra surge for it. The air conditioner is still the starter that counts.

A different pair, Honda's dryer and 8-inch range, already sizes to 11,000 W on backup generator installation. That list does not include the 4-ton air conditioner, and 11,000 W is still above 10,000 W. The general load-list method, without these numbers, stays on whole-house generator sizing.

Transfer-switch class is the running watts, not the 10,000

planSwitch() adds the watts you type and does not add surge. The classes in that function are 30 A = 7,200 W, 50 A = 12,000 W, and 100 A = 24,000 W. The first class at or above the total wins.

  • The 5,192 W running total fits 30 A / 7,200 W.
  • The 9,692 W total does not. 7,200 is smaller than 9,692, so the planner moves to 50 A / 12,000 W.

Dividing the 10,000 W recommendation by 240 V is 41.7 A. That is arithmetic, not a breaker printed on a generator. It is above 30 A. The planner can say 30 A because it looked at 5,192 W of running load, while a 10,000 W, 240 V machine's output current is a larger number than a 30 A inlet. Matching the inlet, the cord, and the switch to the generator is the electrician's step. The planner does not do it.

A common mistake

Calling 10 kW "enough for a house" because the air conditioner alone, or the air conditioner plus a fridge, landed on 10,000 W. The water heater is one published row, and it moves the same formula to 15,500 W. The other mistake is covering Champion's start with a generator whose starting rating stops at 6,250 W. That 6,250 W figure is the Champion manual cited on the 4-ton page, and Champion's own 4-ton start band begins at 6,500 W. A 10,000 W running rating does not repair a starting rating that sits under 8,000 W.

Limits, and when to call a pro

This page does not name a 10 kW model. It names the watt class the formula prints. Your condenser's nameplate can sit anywhere in Champion's band, or outside it. Use the lower end of the band, 4,000 W running and 6,500 W starting, only if that is the row you mean, and run it again on the calculator. Do not average the band.

A hardwired condenser, a water heater, and a transfer switch take a licensed electrician and a permit where local code requires one. Portable placement, if the set is portable, stays outdoors at least 20 feet from the house. That distance is on how to run a generator safely. A 20 kW standby sheet, with its own fuel table, is a different machine: 20 kW home standby.
