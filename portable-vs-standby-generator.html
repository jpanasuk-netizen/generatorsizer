Portable vs standby generator

Home backup vs temporary / job-site use — side-by-side on install, fuel, transfer switch, noise, and when wattage sizing comes first.

Quick answer: Choose portable for short or rare outages, camping / job-site dual use, and a lower cost path with manual start. Choose standby when outages are long or frequent, you need automatic start while away, or you want whole-house loads without cord juggling. Either way, size running + starting watts with the free calculator before you buy.

Who this is for

Home backup: fridge, well pump, furnace, lights, maybe sump — during utility outages. Temporary / job-site: tools, RV, camping, or a unit that also covers the house when storms hit. Portable fits both; standby is fixed home backup only.

When portable wins

Outages are short and occasional. You want one unit for the house and the campsite or job site. Budget and speed matter more than hands-off automation. Pair a correctly sized portable with a transfer switch so you are not running extension cords through a doorway.

When standby wins

You are often away when power fails (freezers, sump, medical loads). Multi-day storms make refueling painful. You need whole-house capacity including central AC without picking circuits. See the whole-house sizing guide for the load-list method.

Wattage still comes first

Portable vs standby is a delivery and install decision — not a substitute for sizing. Add running watts for everything you will power at once, add the extra surge of the largest motor, then buy about 20% headroom. FAQ

Do I need a transfer switch for a portable generator?

Yes if you want to power household circuits safely. A manual transfer switch or interlock keeps utility power and generator power from connecting at once — back-feeding through a dryer outlet is illegal and dangerous. Extension cords alone can run a few appliances; circuits need a transfer switch or interlock path. See the transfer switch sizing guide for 30 A vs 50 A pairing.

Is a standby generator worth it for outages under a day?

Often no. Short, rare outages are where a portable generator plus a transfer switch usually wins on cost path and flexibility. Standby earns its install when outages are long or frequent, you are away from home often, or you need whole-house loads (including central AC) without manual setup.

Can one portable unit run a whole house?

A portable can run critical circuits — fridge, well pump, furnace fan, lights, sump — when sized for running watts plus the biggest starting surge and wired through a transfer switch. Full whole-house including central AC usually needs a larger portable class or a standby path. Size the load list first with the free calculator before you buy.
