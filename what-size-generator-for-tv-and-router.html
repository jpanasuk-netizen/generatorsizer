What Size Generator Do I Need to Run a TV and Router?

Published October 2, 2026.

A television and a router are small loads. Honda lists the same watts for starting and running, and Champion does not list a router start.

Quick answer: Honda lists a 20-inch flat TV at 120 watts starting and running and a 46-inch flat TV at 190 watts starting and running, and Champion lists a router at 10–20 watts with no separate start. There is no extra surge on these rows, so the size that covers them clears 190 watts of running load. A Honda EU2200i rated at 1,800 W covers a TV and router with the maximum still at 2,200 W.

How to size it

Add the running watts of every load that will be on together, then add the single highest starting surge (Generac; Honda). Generac notes that starting watts are often 2–3 times running watts, and that standby units should get a dealer survey.

Add the TV watts and the router watts. A 46-inch set is 190 W and a router is at most 20 W on these charts. Honda notes that an inverter converts AC to DC and back to AC, which gives lower THD than a conventional generator that runs at full RPM. The hour lines on that explainer, and a 500 W walk of this TV and router, are on what an inverter generator is.

FAQ

How many watts is a flat-screen TV?

Honda lists 120 watts starting and running for a 20-inch flat TV and 190 watts starting and running for a 46-inch flat TV.

How many watts does a router use?

Champion lists 10–20 watts, with no separate start.

Should I triple the TV watts for startup?

No. Generac says starts are often 2–3 times running watts when a motor starts. Honda lists the same watts for TV starting and running, and Champion does not list a router start, so do not add a second surge.

Will a small inverter cover the TV?

Yes. 190 W plus a router at 10–20 W is well under the EU2200i rated output of 1,800 W.
