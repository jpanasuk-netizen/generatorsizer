What size generator for a food truck?

Published October 3, 2026.

Built by Jeremy Panasuk.

No truck was tested for this page. The pick is the watt class that clears one published appliance list after this site's formula, not a star rating.

Quick answer: For an Energy Star fridge at the top of Honda's running band, a 4-cup coffee maker, and a 650 W microwave, the calculator recommends 3,500 W. A commercial fryer or griddle is not on that chart. Use the nameplate, or leave it off the list.

The list that is actually on a chart

Food-truck articles often invent a fryer wattage. This page does not. Honda's wattage guide does publish these rows:

Refrigerator or freezer, Energy Star132–1921,200

The worked case uses 192 W running for the fridge, the high end of Honda's range. Honda's microwave row labeled "650 watts" is the oven's cooking rating. The generator sees 1,000 W running and 1,000 W starting. That distinction is already spelled out on the microwave page.

Worked example

Inputs, all on together, headroom 20%, 8 hours a day, 4 gallon tank. Same steps as sizeGenerator() in assets/app.js:

  • Running total = 192 + 600 + 1,000 = 1,792 W.
  • Largest starting wattage is the fridge at 1,200 W, which is higher than the microwave's 1,000 W. Extra surge = 1,200 − 192 = 1,008 W.
  • Peak = 1,792 + 1,008 = 2,800 W.
  • 2,800 × 1.20 = 3,360 W, rounded up to the next 500 W = 3,500 W recommended.
  • Gasoline at 50% of that recommendation = 3,500 / 7,000 = 0.5 gallons per hour. A 4 gallon tank lasts 8.0 hours. Propane at 1.37 times that gasoline rate lasts 5.8 hours on the same 4 gallons. At 8 hours a day the gasoline figure is 4.0 gallons per day. The propane side of that line is on how much propane a generator uses.

So the class that matches this list is a generator whose published running rating clears 3,500 W, with a starting rating that clears the 2,800 W peak. That is the whole "best" claim on this page. Check the model sheet. Do not treat an Amazon title as the spec.

A common mistake

Adding every starting watt (1,200 + 600 + 1,000 = 2,800) and then adding them again on top of the running total double-counts the microwave and the coffee maker. Those two rows already use the same number for start and run. Only the fridge has a real gap between 1,200 and 192, and the formula adds that gap once.

The other mistake is pasting a 240 V electric range onto a food-truck receptacle plan. Honda marks an 8-inch range element at 2,100 W and flags 240 V. This three-item list does not include it.

Limits, and when to call a pro

A health-department truck with a hood, a propane fryer, and an electric refrigerator is an installation, not a tailgate. Hardwiring a generator into the truck, or tying it to a commissary panel, takes a licensed electrician and a permit where local code requires one. Carbon monoxide rules do not relax because the load is a coffee maker. Run the set outdoors, exhaust away from the service window. The placement wording is on how to run a generator safely.

If your equipment nameplate is higher than Honda's row, the nameplate wins. Re-enter those watts on the calculator.
