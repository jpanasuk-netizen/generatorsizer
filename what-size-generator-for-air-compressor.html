What Size Generator Do I Need to Run an Air Compressor?

Published October 2, 2026.

The one-horsepower start is the planning figure. Larger compressors on the same chart jump past it.

Quick answer: Honda lists a 1 hp air compressor at 1,600 running watts and 4,500 starting watts, and Champion lists a 1 hp compressor at 1,500 running watts and 4,500 starting watts. Covering both takes a running rating that clears 1,600 W and a starting rating that clears 4,500 W. Two Honda EU2200i units in parallel reach only up to 4,400 W, short of that start, while the Champion manual lists 6,250 W starting.

How to size it

Add the running watts of every load that will be on together, then add the single highest starting surge (Generac; Honda). Generac notes that starting watts are often 2–3 times running watts, and that standby units should get a dealer survey.

A 1/2 hp compressor is a different row: Honda lists 975 running watts and 1,600 starting watts, which fits the EU2200i rated output of 1,800 W and maximum of 2,200 W. Larger Champion rows do not fit the manual's starting rating: 2 hp is 2,500 W running and 7,500 W starting, and 3 hp is 3,500 W running and 10,500 W starting, both above 6,250 W starting.

FAQ

What starting watts does a 1 hp air compressor need?

Both charts list 4,500 starting watts. Honda lists 1,600 W running and Champion lists 1,500 W running.

Can parallel EU2200i units start a 1 hp compressor?

Parallel output is up to 4,400 W. The start is 4,500 W, so they are short.

What about a 1/2 hp compressor?

Honda lists 975 W running and 1,600 W starting. That fits an EU2200i at 1,800 W rated and 2,200 W maximum.

Do 2 hp and 3 hp compressors fit a 6,250 W start?

No. Champion lists 7,500 W starting for 2 hp and 10,500 W starting for 3 hp. The manual lists 6,250 W starting.
