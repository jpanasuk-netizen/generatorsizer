What Size Generator Do I Need to Run an Electric Range Burner?

Published October 2, 2026.

One element is the load to plan for, not every burner at once.

Quick answer: Honda lists an 8-inch range element at 2,100 running watts and 2,100 starting watts at 240 V, and Champion lists one burner at 1,200–2,400 watts with no separate start. Covering both takes a running rating that clears 2,400 W and a starting rating that clears 2,100 W. A Honda EU2200i rated at 1,800 W does not cover that running load, while two EU2200i units in parallel reach up to 4,400 W and the Champion manual lists 5,000 W running.

How to size it

Add the running watts of every load that will be on together, then add the single highest starting surge (Generac; Honda). Generac notes that starting watts are often 2–3 times running watts, and that standby units should get a dealer survey.

Honda shows the same 2,100 W for start and run, at 240 V. Champion does not list a separate start, so do not add one. A hardwired range needs a transfer switch or interlock installed by an electrician. See the transfer switch sizing guide. The manual lists generator output at 120/240 V.

FAQ

Is 2,100 W the cooking power or the generator load?

On Honda's chart it is both the running and the starting watts of the 8-inch element, at 240 V.

Can one EU2200i run the 8-inch element?

No. The element is 2,100 W continuous. The EU2200i is rated 1,800 W, even though its maximum is 2,200 W.

What if Champion's burner is the one I have?

Champion lists one burner at 1,200–2,400 W with no separate start. Cover 2,400 W running.

Can I turn on every burner?

These rows are one element. Honda lists an 8-inch element at 2,100 W, not a whole oven. Add only the elements that will be on.
