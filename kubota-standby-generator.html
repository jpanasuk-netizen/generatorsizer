Kubota standby generator

Published October 3, 2026.

Built by Jeremy Panasuk.

One sheet, one model. "Standby" on that sheet is a kilowatt rating. It is not a claim that the set starts itself when the utility drops.

Quick answer: Kubota Engine America's GL7000-USA spec sheet (KEAMKT-0306) lists standby output of 7.0 kVA (7.0 kW) and prime output of 6.5 kVA (6.5 kW) at 120/240 V, power factor 1.0. The printed amperage, 54.2 A at 120 V and 27.1 A at 240 V, matches the 6.5 kW prime rating. The sheet also says "Terminal Available." It does not describe an automatic transfer switch.

What the sheet separates

The GL7000-USA is a 60 Hz single-phase diesel generator with a Kubota Z482 engine, fueled with diesel fuel No. 2 (ASTM D975). The generator block prints two electrical outputs: standby 7.0 kVA (7.0 kW) and prime 6.5 kVA (6.5 kW). Farther down, under the engine, a different row says continuous rated output 8.1 kW (10.9 HP). That row sits with bore, stroke, and engine speed (3,600 rpm). It is the engine's continuous rated output on the sheet, not a third generator kilowatt to shop against the 7.0 kW line.

Receptacles printed on the sheet: 5-20RA (GFCI), L5-30R, L6-30R, L14-30R, plus a ground terminal, and the words "Terminal Available." Starting system: electric, 12 volt DC, with a key switch on the control-panel callout. Nothing on the sheet says the unit senses a utility outage and transfers a house. A residential automatic standby is a different product. The portable-versus-automatic distinction is on portable vs standby.

The amp line is the prime rating

6,500 W / 120 V = 54.17 A, which rounds to the printed 54.2 A. 6,500 W / 240 V = 27.08 A, which rounds to the printed 27.1 A. Dividing the standby 7,000 W by those same voltages gives about 58.3 A and 29.2 A. Those currents are not on the sheet. Using 54.2 A as if it were the 7.0 kW standby current overstates what the amp row says. The same power-factor-1.0 reading on a Generac portable sheet, with that sheet's own amps, is on kVA, kW, and amps.

Half-load fuel, quoted, not repaired

At 1/2 load the sheet prints 1.7 L/h (0.45 gal/h) and 16.5 continuous hours. The tank is 7.4 gal. 7.4 / 0.45 = 16.4 hours to one decimal, next to the printed 16.5 hours. The sheet does not state the wattage of that "1/2 load" row, so this page does not invent one.

The full-load row does not agree with itself. It prints 3.6 L/h (0.69 gal/h) and 10.0 hours. 3.6 liters per hour is not 0.69 gallons per hour, and 7.4 / 0.69 is about 10.7 hours, not the printed 10.0. This page leaves that row as printed. It does not publish a corrected diesel rate.

The oil-capacity pair on the same sheet is also internally inconsistent, so it is left off this page.

Worked comparison: a 2,000 W house list is not this tank

On diesel standby generator, Honda's Energy Star fridge at 192 W running / 1,200 W start plus a 1/3 hp furnace fan at 700 W running / 1,400 W start produces a 2,000 W recommendation from this site's portable formula, and a gasoline figure of about 0.286 gal/h at 50% of that recommendation. 2.0 kW is below the GL7000-USA prime rating of 6.5 kW and below the standby rating of 7.0 kW. That is a watt comparison only.

It is not the burn of this diesel set. The sheet's half-load diesel figure is 0.45 gal/h and 16.5 hours on its own 7.4 gal tank, from Kubota's test of the GL7000-USA. It is not a test of an 892 W running house load, and it is not the calculator's gasoline line divided into diesel by EIA's Btu-per-gallon ratio. Replay the house list on the calculator if you want the portable gasoline number. Read the PDF if you want the diesel number.

A common mistake

Seeing "7 kW standby" in the sheet's opening sentence and treating the GL7000-USA as an automatic home standby that starts on its own and feeds the panel. The sheet's own hardware list is a key switch, receptacles, and a terminal. Connecting any of that to a building is the installation problem, not a feature the PDF already solved.

Limits, and when to call a pro

Permanent installation, a transfer switch, and a permit where local code requires one are a licensed electrician's job. A 20 kW air-cooled natural-gas and LP sheet, with its own fuel table, is on 20 kW home standby generators. A service contract and load-bank testing for a stationary set are on finding a commercial generator service. Placement stays outdoors. The agency distances are on how to run a generator safely. This page does not weigh the set, measure the 66 dB(A), or claim a runtime for a house that was not on Kubota's test.
