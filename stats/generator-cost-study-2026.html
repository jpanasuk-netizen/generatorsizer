How Much Does a Generator Cost in 2026? Prices, Fuel Costs & Outage Math

Data study · Published September 12, 2026 · Every number below links to its source: EIA price series, EIA Electric Power Annual 2024, CPSC rulemaking records, and a same-day 43-listing Amazon.com price check.

Key takeaways

  1. Median portable-generator prices (Amazon, Sept 12, 2026, 43 listings): $329 for 2,000–2,500 W inverters, $550 for 3,500–5,000 W, $800 for 5,000–7,000 W, $1,159 for 8,000–12,500 W.
  2. A 7,500 W generator at 50% load costs $13–19 per 5-hour evening outage in gasoline at the September 2026 EIA national average of $4.30/gal.
  3. Run it around the clock and the same unit burns $62–93 of fuel per day.
  4. Generator electricity delivers at $0.69–1.03 per kWh — 3.8–5.6× the grid's 18.34¢/kWh U.S. residential average (June 2026).
  5. U.S. customers averaged 11 hours of power interruptions in 2024 — nearly double the prior decade's average (EIA).
  6. 80% of 2024's outage hours came from three hurricanes: Beryl, Helene, and Milton (EIA).
  7. Portable-generator CO poisoning killed an average of 85 Americans a year in 2017–2019, the worst year being 103 deaths in 2005 (CPSC).
  8. CPSC investigated 1,332 generator CO deaths over 18 years and estimates its CO-shutoff rule will prevent 2,148 deaths over 30 years.
  9. U.S. weekly gasoline peaked at $5.107/gal the week of June 13, 2022 (EIA) — the most expensive fuel a portable generator has ever drunk.
  10. Annual EIA gasoline averages fell from $4.06 (2022) to $3.42 (2024) to $3.22 (2025) — then 2026 weeks have ranged $2.91–$4.63.
  11. Residential propane ran $2.67/gal in its latest reporting week (March 30, 2026), putting a 7,500 W propane-fired unit at $10–17 per 5 hours.
  12. Tri-fuel portables carry a ~38% price premium: $1,249 median vs $902 for dual-fuel across matched listings.
  13. Watts-per-dollar spread at the extremes: a $899 13,000 W DuroStar delivers 14.5 W/$; a $4,999 Honda EU7000iS delivers 1.4 W/$ — a 10× gap.
  14. The cheapest 2,200 W inverter found was $220 — enough for a 150 W refrigerator, lights, phones, and a microwave (the essentials the EIA says most outages interrupt).

How much fuel does a generator burn in a blackout?

At the September 2026 EIA national gasoline average of $4.295/gal (week ending September 7, 2026), a typical 7,500 W portable at 50% load — the load that covers a fridge, freezer, well pump, lights, and electronics — costs $13–19 per 5-hour evening outage and $62–93 per 24 hours, at manufacturer fuel-consumption specs of 0.6–0.9 gal/hr for that class.

The math, so you can redo it with your own numbers: (gallons per hour) × (EIA $/gal) × (hours). We use 0.6 / 0.75 / 0.9 gal/hr as the published spec range for 7,500 W-class portables at 50% load; EIA weekly U.S. retail gasoline $4.295 (Sept 7, 2026). Cross-check any model's spec sheet against our generator sizing calculator, which computes the same figure per load.

Propane prices differently: EIA's residential propane series last reported $2.674/gal (week of March 30, 2026; the series publishes only during heating season, October–March). At the 1.3×-volume de-rate dual-fuel generators suffer on propane (published manufacturer specs; DOE energy content 91,600 vs 125,000 BTU/gal), that's $10–17 per 5-hour outage — cheaper per hour than gasoline at current prices, and storable indefinitely, which is why dual-fuel exists.

Is generator power more expensive than grid power?

Yes — by 3.8–5.6×. Generator-delivered electricity costs $0.69–1.03/kWh at September 2026 prices; the EIA U.S. residential electricity average was 18.34¢/kWh in June 2026 and 17.30¢/kWh for full-year 2025. And grid prices keep climbing: EIA's annual residential averages went 15.04¢ (2022) → 16.00¢ (2023) → 16.48¢ (2024) → 17.30¢ (2025).

That multiple is the honest way to frame the generator-vs-battery decision: fuel is a rent you pay per outage hour, forever. A battery bank sizer shows the capex alternative — and for context, EnergySage's 21st Marketplace Report (H1 2025) put home solar+battery at a record-low median $2.48/W, i.e. $29,016 before incentives for the median 11.7 kW quoted system.

Why did 2024 double the average outage time?

Three hurricanes did it: EIA's Electric Power Annual 2024 reports U.S. customers averaged 11 hours of interruptions in 2024, nearly twice the prior decade's annual average, with Hurricanes Beryl, Helene, and Milton accounting for 80% of all hours without electricity. When comparing years, EIA attributes most differences to major events — which is exactly the tail risk a generator (or battery) is priced against.

How dangerous are portable generators?

Carbon monoxide, not fire or shock, is the killer: generator-related CO deaths averaged 85 per year in the most recent complete CPSC data (2017–2019), the record year was 103 deaths in 2005, and CPSC field staff investigated 1,332 generator CO deaths in total. CPSC's proposed safety standard estimates it would prevent 2,148 deaths over 30 years (about 72 per year) — over $1 billion a year in avoided deaths and injuries, or roughly $273 of benefit per generator sold.

Primary source: CPSC Notice of Proposed Rulemaking, "Safety Standard for Portable Generators," 88 FR, April 20, 2023, Federal Register document 2023-07870.

Is a tri-fuel generator worth the premium?

Only if natural gas is your plan: tri-fuel portables listed at a $1,249 median vs $902 for dual-fuel (gas+propane) in matched listings — a 38% premium for the NG hose and fitting. Gasoline-only remains cheapest to buy but worst to store (3–12 months with stabilizer per manufacturer guidance), propane stores indefinitely, and a natural-gas hookup is the only fuel that never goes stale in your garage.
