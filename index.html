The generator-size form opens with two loads: Refrigerator (Energy Star, 20 cu ft) at 150 running watts and 600 starting watts, and LED lights (whole home) at 80 running watts and 80 starting watts. How does generator sizing work?

Three numbers decide what you buy. Running watts — the sum of everything switched on at the same time. Starting watts — the brief spike when a motor kicks on; only the largest one counts at any instant, because motors don't all start together. Headroom — the 20% margin on the form, so the generator is not pinned at its rated output, and so a load you forgot still fits.

Frequently asked questions

What size generator do I need to run a house?

Add the running watts of everything you will power at once, add the extra surge of the largest motor load, then buy about 20% above that total. The 20% is the headroom already filled in on the calculator form on this page. Which appliances stay on decides the class. Put nameplate watts in the form.

How do starting watts differ from running watts?

Running watts are what an appliance draws continuously. Starting (surge) watts are the brief spike when a motor starts. Your generator's surge rating must cover the biggest starting load on top of everything else already running. Read starting watts on the nameplate or on a page that cites the manufacturer, not from a blanket multiple.

How long will a generator run on a tank of gas?

Runtime depends on the tank and the load. The calculator on this page applies its own fuel rule and prints the result after you size the list. EIA gasoline and propane heat contents are in the fuel section on this page. The calculator's propane volume factor is on the propane guide.

Do I need a transfer switch to run a generator?

Yes, if you are connecting to household circuits. Code requires a manual or automatic transfer switch. Back-feeding through a dryer outlet is illegal and can kill utility workers. The watt ceiling for a 30 A or 50 A switch, shown as amps times 240 V, is on the transfer-switch guide.

Why does the calculator add 20% headroom?

The 20% buffer covers two things the wattage list can't predict: measured loads running above their nameplate rating, and future appliances you will add later. It is headroom on the generator, not permission to exceed its rating. Sustained overload still trips the breaker or stalls the engine.
