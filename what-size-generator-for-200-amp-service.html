What size whole house generator for a 200 amp service?

Published November 17, 2026.

The 200 A rating is the size of the panel, not the size of the load. Add up what you will actually run, then size above that.

Quick answer: A 200 amp, 240 volt service can carry up to 48,000 watts, but that is the panel's ceiling, not your outage load (200 × 240, derived). Generac says an 18 to 22 kW unit suits a mid-sized 2,000 sq ft home, and recommends sizing 10% to 20% above the calculated load in kW.

48,000 W is 200 A × 240 V, plain arithmetic on this page. The 18 to 22 kW line is under Generac's “Standby Generator for 2,000 Sq. Ft. Home” heading, read November 17, 2026. The 10% to 20% line is from the residential bullet of the Generac Standby Generator Sizing Guide 2025, read the same day.

Panel ceiling versus real load

A 200 A main breaker at 240 V sets the most the service can carry before the breaker trips: 200 × 240 = 48,000 W. Homes on a 200 A service rarely run everything at once. The generator only has to carry the loads you choose to back up, which is why the calculator below starts from a load list and not from the panel rating.

Generac's sizing article puts an 18 kW to 22 kW unit as “the sweet spot” for a 2,000 sq ft home. The Generac sizing guide says to add 10% to 20% to the total calculated load in kW for residential and light commercial jobs. A dealer load survey is still the step before buying a standby unit; the transfer switch side of that is on transfer switch sizing.

Load list against the 48,000 W ceiling

Pick the essentials list or the whole-home list, then edit any watts or untick a row. Every prefilled row is the top of the range on Champion's generator wattage chart, read November 17, 2026. The running total adds the running watts. Peak adds the single largest extra starting surge on top, the same method as the whole-house sizing guide. Headroom then adds the 10% to 20% from the Generac guide; the kW line rounds up to one decimal.
