Best 10 portable generators for home backup

Ranked by the sizing math, not brand loyalty: surge rating against real startup loads, fuel burn at 50% load, power quality (THD), and fuel flexibility. Find your wattage class first with the size calculator, then buy inside it.

Affiliate slot 1 · 2,000–2,500 W dual-fuel inverter

1. Best for fridge & freezer only — 2,000–2,500 W dual-fuel inverter

~1,800–2,200 running watts, 2,000–2,500 surge, under 50 lb, ~0.3 gal/hr at typical food-preservation loads. The right tool for the most common backup job; quiet enough to run overnight in a neighborhood. A 20 lb propane tank runs it roughly 8–10 hours at half load and stores for years.

Affiliate slot 2 · 4,500 W dual-fuel

2. Best for essentials + well pump — 4,500 W dual-fuel class

~4,500 running / 5,500+ surge handles a 1/2 HP well pump starting against a fridge, furnace fan, and lights — the classic rural-water-home load (~1,900 W running, ~3,200 W peak with LRA margin). Check for a 240 V outlet if your pump is 240 V; that single spec decides returns more than any other.

Affiliate slot 3 · 7,500 W open-frame dual-fuel

3. Best for whole-house essentials — 7,500 W open-frame dual-fuel

~9,000–9,500 surge covers a central AC start plus the essentials board. Louder than inverters, but the price-per-watt leader and the last one standing in a multi-day outage. Pair with a 50 A manual transfer switch (12,000 W capacity) for direct-panel wiring.

Affiliate slot 4 · 3,000 W premium inverter

4. Best premium quiet pick — 3,000 W premium inverter

The class that defined quiet backup: ~2,800 running / 3,000+ surge, THD under 3%, eco-throttle that sips fuel at fridge-only loads. Premium price is the tax; you pay for sound levels and electronics-safe power, not raw watts.

Affiliate slot 5 · 2,200 W value inverter

5. Best budget inverter — 2,200 W value class

The sub-$600 inverter tier from established brands — same clean power and parallel capability as the premium class, a bit heavier and louder. The value pick for the fridge-and-freezer food-preservation job.

Affiliate slot 6 · 3,600 W dual-fuel inverter

6. Best RV/backup crossover — 3,600 W dual-fuel inverter

~3,600 running / 4,500 surge with 30 A RV outlet built in. Between outage duties it's the camper's AC-ready power plant; one unit, two jobs.

Affiliate slot 7 · tri-fuel

7. Best tri-fuel — 4,500 W gas / propane / natural gas

Runs on the hose off your home's natural gas line — unlimited runtime during a gas outage that leaves the grid down. Power output drops ~10% on propane and ~15% on natural gas versus gasoline; size accordingly.

Affiliate slot 8 · 5,000 W large inverter

8. Best large inverter — 5,000 W class

Inverter power quality at whole-essentials scale: central AC (with soft start) or well pump plus everything, with clean sine power for the fridge's control board. Heavier and pricier than conventional 5 kW units — that's the trade.

Affiliate slot 9 · 7,000 W conventional

9. Best job-site/backup hybrid — 7,000 W conventional

Contractor-grade frames with GFCI protection that also serve home backup. Not electronics-quiet, not THD-clean — fine for fridges with surge protectors, tools, and heaters, less ideal for sensitive loads.

Affiliate slot 10 · 9,000–12,000 W dual-fuel

10. Best near-standby — 9,000–12,000 W dual-fuel

The portable ceiling: 12,000+ surge starts nearly anything, feeds a 50 A switch, and backs up most of a house short of electric heat. Fuel burn at 50% load (~1.2–1.5 gal/hr gas) makes the dual-fuel option and a 100 lb propane setup worth pricing in.

How the ranking was made

Every slot is a class, not a single model, because inventory rotates weekly — the sizing math doesn't. Within each class, compare four manufacturer-published numbers: surge watts (must exceed your worst startup — well pump owners, read this first), runtime at 25–50% load (where home backup actually lives), THD ≤ 5% for anything feeding electronics, and 240 V output if you have a well pump or want transfer-switch wiring. Verify the model's current spec sheet before buying; ratings drift between revisions.

Bought the generator? Two accessories that matter more than the upgrade wattage

A manual transfer switch or interlock kit (code-required for panel connection) and a quality 12-gauge cord set. Both are affiliate slots 11–12 in practice.
