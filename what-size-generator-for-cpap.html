What Size Generator Do I Need to Run a CPAP?

Published October 2, 2026.

No surge is listed for a CPAP, so this page does not invent one.

Quick answer: Champion lists a CPAP at 30–60 watts and a heated CPAP at 60–120 watts, and it does not list a starting surge. Plan on a running rating that clears 120 W and do not add a surge the chart does not show. A Honda EU2200i is rated 1,800 W with a 2,200 W maximum and is listed at 48–57 dB(A), so the watts fit and the spec sheet is the quiet-inverter reference.

How to size it

Add the running watts of every load that will be on together, then add the single highest starting surge (Generac; Honda). Generac notes that starting watts are often 2–3 times running watts, and that standby units should get a dealer survey.

The EU2200i spec sheet also lists 47.4 lb, 3.2 hours at rated load, and 8.1 hours at 1/4 load. A CPAP at 30–60 W, or 60–120 W heated, is under the 1,800 W rated output, so this page does not invent a longer runtime than those published hours. Honda notes that an inverter converts AC to DC and back to AC, which gives lower THD than a conventional generator that runs at full RPM. The machine still stays outdoors.

FAQ

Does a CPAP have a starting surge?

Champion does not list one. The rows are 30–60 W and 60–120 W heated. Do not multiply them.

Why mention the EU2200i?

The watts are small. The EU2200i is the quiet inverter in the source list: 48–57 dB(A), 1,800 W rated, 2,200 W maximum, 47.4 lb.

How long does that spec sheet say it runs?

Honda lists 3.2 hours at rated load and 8.1 hours at 1/4 load. Those are the published runtimes, not a CPAP-only estimate.

Can the CPAP sit next to the generator?

No. CPSC says to run a generator outdoors only, 20 ft from the house. Use an outdoor-rated cord to reach the machine.
