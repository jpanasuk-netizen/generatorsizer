What Size Generator Do I Need to Run a 13,500 BTU RV Air Conditioner?

Roof air conditioning is the load that sets an RV generator. This page is only that question.

Full breakdown: RV generator sizing.

Quick answer: Honda lists a 13,500 BTU RV air conditioner at 1,800 running watts and 2,800 starting watts, and Champion lists a 13.5k BTU RV air conditioner at 1,500–2,000 running watts and 2,800–3,500 starting watts. Covering both takes a running rating that clears 2,000 W and a starting rating that clears 3,500 W. A single Honda EU2200i, rated 1,800 W with a 2,200 W maximum, matches Honda's 1,800 W running figure but does not cover Champion's 2,000 W running figure or a 3,500 W start, while two EU2200i units in parallel reach up to 4,400 W and the Champion manual lists 5,000 W running and 6,250 W starting.

How to size it

Add the running watts of every load that will be on together, then add the single highest starting surge (Generac; Honda). Generac notes that starting watts are often 2–3 times running watts, and that standby units should get a dealer survey.

This page is the one air conditioner, not the whole coach. Add a microwave or another load only after the air conditioner start is covered. Honda lists a 1,000 W microwave at 1,500 W running and starting, which would be its own running load on top of the air conditioner.

FAQ

Can one EU2200i run a 13,500 BTU RV AC?

Not if you cover both charts. The EU2200i is 1,800 W rated and 2,200 W maximum. Honda lists 1,800 W running and 2,800 W starting. Champion goes to 2,000 W running and 3,500 W starting.

Do two EU2200i units cover it?

Parallel output is up to 4,400 W, which is above a 3,500 W start and a 2,000 W running load.

What is the difference between the Honda and Champion rows?

Honda is a single pair, 2,800 W start and 1,800 W run. Champion is a range, 2,800–3,500 W start and 1,500–2,000 W run. Cover the top of each.

Where is the 15,000 BTU air conditioner?

On its own page. Honda lists that size at 3,300 W starting and 2,000 W running.
