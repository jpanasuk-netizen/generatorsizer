What Size Generator Do I Need to Run a 15,000 BTU RV Air Conditioner?

A larger roof air conditioner needs a higher start. This page is only that question.

Full breakdown: RV generator sizing.

Quick answer: Honda lists a 15,000 BTU RV air conditioner at 2,000 running watts and 3,300 starting watts, and Champion lists a 15k BTU unit at 2,000–2,500 running watts and 3,500–4,500 starting watts. Covering both takes a running rating that clears 2,500 W and a starting rating that clears 4,500 W. Two Honda EU2200i units in parallel reach only up to 4,400 W, which is short of that 4,500 W start, while the Champion manual lists 5,000 W running and 6,250 W starting.

How to size it

Add the running watts of every load that will be on together, then add the single highest starting surge (Generac; Honda). Generac notes that starting watts are often 2–3 times running watts, and that standby units should get a dealer survey.

A single EU2200i is rated 1,800 W with a 2,200 W maximum, which is below both the 2,000 W running figure and the 3,300 W start. The smaller 13,500 BTU roof air conditioner is a separate page.

FAQ

Can parallel EU2200i generators run a 15,000 BTU RV AC?

They reach up to 4,400 W. Champion's upper start is 4,500 W, so the parallel figure is short of the chart that covers both sources.

What does Honda list for this air conditioner?

2,000 running watts and 3,300 starting watts.

What does Champion list?

2,000–2,500 running watts and 3,500–4,500 starting watts for a 15k BTU RV air conditioner.

Which published generator rating covers both?

The Champion manual lists 5,000 W running and 6,250 W starting, which is above 2,500 W running and 4,500 W starting.
