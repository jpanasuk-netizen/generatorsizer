What Size Generator Do I Need to Run a Microwave?

Published October 2, 2026.

Honda lists three cooking sizes, and the largest one sets the generator.

Quick answer: Honda lists a 1,000 W microwave at 1,500 running watts and 1,500 starting watts (a 650 W oven is 1,000 W and an 800 W oven is 1,300 W), and Champion lists 600–1,200 watts with no separate start. Covering the 1,000 W oven takes ratings that clear 1,500 W running and 1,500 W starting. A Honda EU2200i rated at 1,800 W, with a 2,200 W maximum, covers that oven.

How to size it

Add the running watts of every load that will be on together, then add the single highest starting surge (Generac; Honda). Generac notes that starting watts are often 2–3 times running watts, and that standby units should get a dealer survey.

Honda lists the same number for start and run on each microwave, so do not add a second surge. Champion does not list a separate start either. Honda notes that an inverter converts AC to DC and back to AC, which gives lower THD than a conventional generator that runs at full RPM.

FAQ

How many watts does a 1,000 W microwave pull from the generator?

Honda lists 1,500 running watts and 1,500 starting watts. The 1,000 W figure is the cooking rating, not the generator load.

What about a smaller microwave?

Honda lists a 650 W microwave at 1,000 W running and starting, and an 800 W microwave at 1,300 W running and starting.

Is Champion's range a start or a run figure?

The chart lists 600–1,200 and no separate start. Treat it as running watts and do not invent a surge.

Can the EU2200i run the largest oven on this chart?

Yes. 1,500 W is under the EU2200i rated output of 1,800 W and under the 2,200 W maximum.
