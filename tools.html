Lab tools

Updated October 3, 2026.

Built by Jeremy Panasuk.

Five calculators and a method page. Each tool page states its formula, the constants, and a worked click. This page is only the index.

Load sequencer

Splits a list into always-on, wait, and never-overlap, then sizes the worst phase plus the headroom you type. Use it when a single sum assumes the microwave starts with the well.

Altitude + heat derate

Turns a nameplate into usable watts with this site’s curve. Use it after you have a watt need, and read the Generac and Honda sentences on that page before you treat the curve as their sheet.

72-hour storm runbook

Prints 24/48/72 hour fuel from running watts, plus a pre-storm, during, and after checklist using CPSC, Ready.gov, FEMA, and OSHA wording. Use it when you are staging cans, not while you are still picking a watt class.

5-year ownership cost

Prices a portable’s purchase, gasoline, and the script’s oil charge against a standby’s install and yearly service. Use your own outage hours. Standby fuel is not in that total.

Lot-line noise

Applies free-field spreading, 20·log10 of the distance ratio, from a reference dBA. The cited reference in the menu is Generac’s 67 dB(A) at 23 ft for the 22 kW row. The other three menu levels are labels stored in the script.

Worked houses + method

States the coincidence idea and replays three stored house stacks. Open it when you want the method beside the tools, not a sixth calculator.

Which one to open

Start with the home calculator or the sequencer if the question is watts. Open derate when the pad is above 1,000 ft or the inlet air is above 77°F. Open the runbook when the watt number is already chosen and you need cans and the placement checklist. Open five-year cost when the question is dollars over time. Open lot-line noise when the question is decibels at a distance you can type.

Connecting a generator to a building takes a licensed electrician and a permit where local code requires one. These pages do not pull that permit.

Sources

The formulas and the worked clicks are on the tool pages. The citations those pages use:

  • Load sequencer — Honda wattage guide for the rows that match it; the three lanes are the script
  • Altitude derate — Generac GP6500/GP8000E manual and Honda EU2200i manual, set next to this site’s curve
  • Storm runbook — CPSC, Ready.gov, FEMA, OSHA 3286, EIA heat content, Honda and Generac oil schedules
  • 5-year cost — the same fuel rule and oil clock as the runbook
  • Lot-line noise — OSHA Technical Manual inverse-square statement, Generac 22 kW sound row, FHWA barrier range
