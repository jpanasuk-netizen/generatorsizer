What size generator for an RV?

30A vs 50A reality, common loads (AC, microwave, converter), quiet inverter vs open-frame — then check your list on the free calculator.

Quick answer: No-AC basics often fit a quiet 2,000–2,200 W inverter. One roof AC on a 30 amp coach usually needs a 3,000–3,600 W class (or soft-start + smaller inverter). Dual-AC / 50 amp loads need a larger class — always size running watts + the biggest compressor surge + ~20% headroom on the free calculator.

RV electrical reality (30A vs 50A)

A 30 amp RV uses one 120 V leg — about 3,600 W through the shore cord. Buying a generator far above that does not help through the TT-30 path. A 50 amp RV has two 120 V legs and a much higher theoretical ceiling, but real simultaneous loads (two ACs + appliances) still need a wattage list — amp rating alone is not a generator size. Quiet inverter vs open-frame

Campground noise rules and neighbors favor inverter generators. Open-frame units often deliver more watts per pound for dual-AC 50 amp coaches. Match class after sizing — then browse the best portable generators list by wattage tier, not by marketing labels.

Headroom, altitude, and heat

Add ~20% headroom above peak (running + largest surge). Generators lose capacity at altitude and in high heat — if you boondock in mountains or desert summers, size up a class or derate per the manufacturer's guidance. Propane dual-fuel modes typically deliver fewer watts than gasoline; check the propane rating against your AC start.

FAQ

Can a 2000W inverter run RV AC?

Usually not reliably with other loads. Roof AC compressors spike well above running watts at start. A 2,000–2,200 W inverter can cover lights, fridge, converter, and microwave without AC. For AC, plan a larger inverter class or a soft-start kit on the AC — then verify with your nameplate and the free calculator.

Do I need dual fuel on the road?

Dual fuel (gas/propane) helps for boondocking when you can tap the coach propane tank and avoid extra gas jugs. Expect some wattage de-rate on propane — check the model's propane rating clears your AC or microwave surge, not only the gasoline rating.

What size generator for a 30 amp vs 50 amp RV?

A 30 amp RV shore cord is limited to about 3,600 W (120 V × 30 A), so a 3,000–3,600 W generator matches the pedestal ceiling. A 50 amp coach has two legs and a higher theoretical ceiling; dual-AC real loads often land in a mid-to-large portable class — size to your simultaneous loads, not the nameplate amp alone.
